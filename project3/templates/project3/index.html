Task 1 · the classifier

TF-IDF over unigrams and bigrams, then multinomial logistic regression. Topic in a news snippet is carried almost entirely by vocabulary, so a linear model on n-grams is the right inductive bias rather than a compromise — and it returns the probabilities every later task needs.

Task 3 · learning to defer

Defer when the expert is likelier to be right than the classifier: P(expert correct | x) > P(classifier correct | x).

Estimating the two competences

SignalWhat it estimatesTest AUC

Text model of expert correctness P(expert correct) {{ quality.expert_competence_auc|floatformat:3 }}

A softmax maximum is not a probability of being right, and the rule compares it against the expert's competence, so it is Platt-scaled onto that scale first. The calibrated confidence is measurably the better of the two estimates, and swapping in the weaker text model costs almost the whole benefit — the team falls to {{ policies.competence_text_zero_margin.team_accuracy|floatformat:4 }}, barely above the classifier alone.

Results, and whether the deferrals were the right ones

Team accuracy alone hides whether the deferrals were well chosen: a rule that defers everything just inherits the expert's accuracy. Each rule is also scored against the oracle decision — defer iff the expert is right here and the classifier wrong — and by what it bought: articles rescued from a wrong classifier answer against those spoiled by handing over a right one.

Task 4 · active learning for expert competence

No expert labels to begin with. {{ active_settings.batch }} queries per round for {{ active_settings.rounds }} rounds, from a pool of {{ active_settings.pool_size }} unlabelled articles.

The system does not need a good model of the expert everywhere — it needs one binary decision to come out right. So the labels worth buying are those near the boundary where the two competences cross. On its own that fails, and the failure is measurable: querying only near the boundary makes the sample unrepresentative and the fitted model does not transfer. The strategy proposed here splits every batch, half uniform and half on the boundary, which repairs it.

The honest conclusion. On this problem active learning does not beat uniform sampling on team accuracy. This expert's competence is defined by topic and topic is easy to read off the text, so a few hundred labels drawn anywhere already characterise it; and the deferral decision only changes about {{ policies.competence_zero_margin.deferral_rate|floatformat:2 }} of answers, so team accuracy is insensitive to a better competence model. Where the targeted strategies do differ is in how well their competence model generalises — and pure boundary sampling is measurably worse than doing nothing clever at all.
